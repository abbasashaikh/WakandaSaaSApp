Three layers — like a restaurant
Every well-built app has three clear layers. Ours does too. Each layer only talks to the one next to it — nobody jumps the chain.
Why separate layers?
Like a restaurant — the customer doesn't walk into the kitchen. The kitchen doesn't call the supplier. Everyone has their lane. Changing one layer doesn't break the others.
Slide 3 of 10 — The star innovation
The secret weapon — a robot driver
Google has no public API for its best AI model. So instead of waiting, we built a robot that drives Google's website automatically.
Out-of-box thinking — this is the clever part
Normally apps connect to a service through an "API" — like a formal phone line. Google's Flow AI has no such line. So we do something unexpected: we launch a real Chrome browser in the background, log in with a real Google account, type the prompt on the actual website, and read back whatever Google shows us. The user sees none of this — it all happens in 30–90 seconds behind the scenes.
Like a remote control car
We steer the browser: open this page, type this text, click this button, read what came back. Repeat for every user request.
Why this matters commercially
We use Google's most powerful model — same one paying Google customers use — without a separate licence or API cost. The only cost is the Google account subscription.
Slide 4 of 10
One request, 8 steps
From the moment you press Send to seeing your image on screen — every step that happens in order.
1
You type a prompt in the app and press Send
2
App sends to server through the secure public tunnel (ngrok)
3
Server checks identity — is your JWT token valid? (hotel key card check)
4
Job added to queue — like taking a numbered ticket at a deli counter
5
Robot browser opens Google Flow, types your prompt, clicks generate
6
Google AI creates the image or video — 30–90 seconds
7
Server downloads and saves the file permanently (URL expiry protection)
8
Your app sees it's ready and shows the result on screen
Polling
Why does the app keep checking?
Generation takes time. The app checks every 3 seconds — like checking your food delivery app until the driver arrives.
Retry logic
What if step 5 fails?
The job retries automatically up to 3 times with increasing delays. If Google is slow on the first try, the second attempt usually works perfectly.
Slide 5 of 10
The waiting room — the job queue
What happens when 5 people press Send at exactly the same time? They get a numbered ticket. The system serves them one by one in order.
Redis
The ticket board
Redis is like the whiteboard in a restaurant kitchen. Every incoming order is written up. Workers pick one at a time. Nothing gets lost even if the server restarts.
BullMQ
The queue manager
BullMQ is the headwaiter — assigns jobs to workers, retries failed ones (up to 3 times), and gives every job a status you can track in real time.
Slide 6 of 10
The filing cabinet — the database
Every user, every job, every result is remembered. Like a very organised filing cabinet that never forgets — even when the app restarts.
SQLite
One file, zero setup
The entire database lives in a single file on disk — like one Excel spreadsheet. No separate database server needed. Handles hundreds of concurrent users reliably with WAL mode enabled.
Slide 7 of 10
The hotel key card — security
You show your booking confirmation once at the reception desk. They give you a key card. You use the key card for every door after that — not the confirmation.
JWT
Short-lived token = safer
Even if someone steals the token, it expires in 1 hour. The license key itself never travels after login. Tokens are stored only in memory, never written to disk.
Admin panel
Multiple users, multiple keys
Each user gets their own license key. Admins can add new users, revoke access instantly, and see a full audit log of every generation.
Slide 8 of 10
The photograph — saving media forever
Google's image links expire after 20 minutes — like a cinema ticket going invalid after the show. We solve this by taking a permanent copy the moment the image is created.
Problem
Expiring links
Google stores images on a temporary CDN (delivery network) with an expiry timestamp in the URL. After 20 minutes, the image becomes unavailable — even for the user who created it.
Solution
Download immediately, serve forever
The moment Google returns a URL, our server downloads the binary file and saves it locally. Users get a permanent local URL. Files auto-clean after 7 days to save disk space.
Slide 9 of 10
The postal tunnel — going public
Your backend runs on your PC at home. But testers are in different cities. ngrok builds an encrypted tunnel so anyone on the internet can reach your local server.
The postbox analogy
ngrok puts a public postbox (URL) on the internet. Any request dropped in is forwarded through a private encrypted tunnel directly to your machine. The URL is public — the channel is secret.
Note
URL changes on restart
ngrok's free tier gives a new URL every session. For a permanent URL, we move the backend to a server with a fixed IP. The app itself never needs rebuilding — just update the URL.
